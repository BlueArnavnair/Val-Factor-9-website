STRUCTURE–FUNCTION ATLAS · HEMOPHILIA B

Severity isn’t about how big the substitution is. It’s about where it lands.

An interactive atlas of 4,713 Factor IX (F9) gene variants, mapped onto the protein’s structure to test one hypothesis: clinical severity in Hemophilia B tracks the structural importance of the mutation site — catalytic residues, disulfide bridges, domain interfaces — more than the physical size of the amino-acid swap itself.

CORE FINDING

Clinical severity in Hemophilia B correlates less with how large the substituted side chain is, and more with whether the mutation lands on a catalytic triad residue, a disulfide-bonded cysteine, or a domain interface.

Tested here across 4,713 logged variant reports spanning the signal peptide, propeptide, Gla domain, EGF1, EGF2, the activation peptide/linker region, and the catalytic serine protease domain of Factor IX (structural context from PDB 1CFH and 6MV4 — see Structure section below for exactly what each covers).

METHODOLOGY

How this atlas is built

A short account of the data pipeline behind the numbers above.

01

Normalize the record

Raw variant reports are parsed, domain labels are standardized against the five major Factor IX regions, and clinical severity is bucketed into Severe / Moderate / Mild / Unclassified.

02

Score the substitution

Each missense change is evaluated for charge, polarity, and side-chain size delta. Disulfide and catalytic-triad flags are only applied when the wild-type residue at that position is independently confirmed to be the expected amino acid (e.g. a flagged disulfide position must actually be a cysteine in this dataset) — a position number alone is not treated as sufficient evidence.

03

Map to structure

Residue numbers are checked against two real experimental structures where coverage exists: PDB 1CFH (Gla domain, NMR) and PDB 6MV4 (EGF2 + catalytic protease domain, 1.37Å X-ray). No single solved structure currently covers the full protein, and this atlas says so rather than implying one does — see the Structure section for exact coverage and numbering caveats.

04

Nomenclature

Variants follow standard HGVS notation throughout: c. for coding-DNA change and p. for the predicted protein consequence, so entries can be mapped directly onto external references (ClinVar, dbSNP, EAHAD) without translation.

05

Why 8 regions, not 4

Mature, circulating Factor IX has 4 core functional domains — Gla, EGF1, EGF2, and the catalytic serine protease domain. This atlas additionally tracks the signal peptide, propeptide, and activation peptide as distinct regions, since variants in these transiently-present, processing-stage segments are clinically reported and structurally meaningful even though they're cleaved away in the final circulating protein. That yields 8 tracked regions in total, not a contradiction of the 4-domain mature structure.

06

Silent variants

Synonymous (silent) substitutions don't change the protein sequence, so their severity — which in the underlying reports ranges from Mild to Severe — can't be explained by the structural model used here. Where a variant is silent, its card says so explicitly and names the transcript-level mechanisms (cryptic splice sites, mRNA secondary structure, codon usage bias) that could plausibly account for it instead.

07

Cysteine-gain variants

154 variants introduce a new cysteine where none existed before (e.g. Trp240Cys). An unpaired, non-disulfide-bonded cysteine is a distinct, well-documented pathogenic mechanism — it can form aberrant disulfide bonds or trigger ER quality-control degradation — and is now flagged separately from cysteine loss.

08

5′ UTR & promoter context

Non-coding 5′ UTR variants are checked against the literature-characterized Factor IX promoter: the Leyden-specific region (−34 to +19) and the experimentally validated LF-A1/HNF4 & androgen-receptor binding oligonucleotide (−40 to −9) implicated in hemophilia B Leyden (Crossley & Brownlee 1990; Crossley et al., Science 1992; OMIM *300746), plus the core Kozak consensus (−6 to −1). Variants outside these characterized elements are labeled as such rather than left unexplained.

09

Identity over position

Every structural flag in this atlas (disulfide, catalytic, cysteine-gain) is checked against the actual wild-type amino acid at that residue, not inferred from a position number alone. This process went through two corrections during development: an initial claim of "His233/Asp282/Ser411" was disproven (233 is Alanine here), a revised "His282/Ser411" was also incomplete (282 is genuinely Histidine but not the true triad member), and the final, fully verified triad — His267 / Asp315 / Ser411 — was confirmed by applying the classic His221/Asp269/Ser365 numbering with the same +46 residue offset independently confirmed via Ser365→Ser411.

10

Real structural metrics (EGF2 / protease domain)

PDB 6MV4 (Vadivel et al. 2019) uses its own numbering internally — classic chymotrypsin numbering with insertion codes (e.g. His57/Asp102/Ser195 for the triad), not this dataset's numbering. A residue-by-residue mapping was built by globally aligning this dataset's consensus wild-type sequence against 6MV4's real chain sequences (Biopython pairwise alignment), then verified against all three known triad anchors before use. This yielded a complete, gap-free map for the Serine Protease domain (235/235 positions) and a near-complete map for EGF2 (41/43 positions).

11

Distance & solvent accessibility

Using that verified map, every covered residue now has a real Cα–Cα distance to the catalytic Ser411 (computed directly from 6MV4's atomic coordinates) and a real solvent accessibility value (freesasa). These replace guesswork with measured numbers for 2,662 of 4,713 variants — those in the EGF2 and Serine Protease domains. The remaining ~2,050 variants (Gla domain, EGF1, signal peptide, propeptide, activation peptide, linker) still have no verified structural coordinates in this atlas.

12

What was measured vs. modeled

Distance and solvent accessibility (items 10–11) are geometric measurements on the wild-type structure only. ΔΔG (item 13) is a genuine energetic prediction from an actual mutant model, computed by FoldX, not estimated. H-bond counts (item 14) are a real geometric measurement, not a rule-of-thumb. None of this is a replacement for wet-lab validation (expressed-protein activity assays) — it's computational evidence, clearly labeled as such throughout.

13

ΔΔG stability (FoldX)

FoldX BuildModel ΔΔG values are now included for 2,007 of 2,115 actionable missense variants in the EGF2/protease domains, run on a RepairPDB-processed 6MV4. Validated three ways before use: values correlate with clinical severity (mean ΔΔG rises from 2.5 kcal/mol in Mild to 7.7 in Severe), disulfide-loss variants are far more destabilizing on average (13.7 vs 4.2 kcal/mol), and the single highest value (Gly412Trp, 62.3 kcal/mol) is a fully buried glycine replaced by a bulky tryptophan — exactly the kind of substitution expected to produce an extreme result. 108 mutations failed to compute; the cause is still being investigated rather than assumed.

14

Hydrogen bonding

Two distinct H-bond quantities are shown, and they are not the same thing. "Wild-type H-bonds" is a real geometric count — polar contacts within 3.5Å measured directly on the actual 6MV4 structure, verified against the catalytic triad (His267/Asp315 both show real, non-zero contact counts, as their biochemical role requires). "Mutant capacity" is a static chemistry lookup (e.g. Arg=5, Val=0) describing the introduced residue's theoretical donor/acceptor count — not a 3D measurement, and shown separately so the two are never confused.

This is a research and educational tool, not a diagnostic one. Severity classifications, structural flags, and mechanism text are generated from aggregated variant reports and standard biochemical rules of thumb — they are not a substitute for clinical genetic counselling or a curated reference database such as the EAHAD Coagulation Factor Variant Databases. Residue numbering follows the precursor-inclusive scheme used in the source dataset and may differ from mature-chain numbering used in some literature.
